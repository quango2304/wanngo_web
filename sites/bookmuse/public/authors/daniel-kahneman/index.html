Daniel Kahneman

Psychologist and Nobel laureate in economics · 1934–2024

Daniel Kahneman was a psychologist who grew up in Paris and moved with his family to what became Israel as a teenager. With his colleague Amos Tversky, he studied how people really make choices, and showed that we often use mental shortcuts that lead us to predictable mistakes. Their work on prospect theory helped start the field of behavioral economics, and Kahneman shared the 2002 Nobel Prize in economics for it. He taught for many years, including at Princeton University. His book Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011) explains much of this research for general readers.
