Oliver Sacks

British neurologist and writer · 1933–2015

Oliver Sacks was a neurologist who was born in London and studied medicine at Oxford. He moved to New York in the 1960s and worked for many years with patients in hospitals and homes there, and later taught at Columbia University and New York University. He became known for writing long, careful stories about his patients instead of short case notes. His book Awakenings (1973) told how patients with a sleeping sickness woke up after a new drug, and The Man Who Mistook His Wife for a Hat (1985) made him famous around the world. The New York Times called him the poet laureate of medicine. He died in New York in 2015.
