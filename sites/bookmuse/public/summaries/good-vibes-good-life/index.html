Good Vibes, Good Life

A summary of the book by Vex King

8 lessons · 18 min · quiz and takeaways

Read it lesson by lessonGet the app

Love yourself, lift your mood

Vex King argues that loving yourself, which means accepting who you are while you keep growing, lifts your mood, and that your mood decides what comes back to you.

King wrote this book in 2018. As a child he spent three years with no fixed home, and he says those hard years taught him what he shares. He says he is not a scientist or a psychologist. Some of his ideas are spiritual beliefs with no scientific proof. Others are plain, useful habits. Take what helps you.

What you’ll learn

Then a quiz, and the key takeaways.

Self-love is two things

Self-love means accepting yourself as you are, knowing you deserve better, and working toward it.

Many people hear “self-love” and think it means being fine with everything. King says it has two parts that must stay in balance. The first is unconditional love for yourself, which is about mindset. You will not love yourself more after you lose weight or have cosmetic surgery. You may feel more confident, but true self-love means valuing who you are now, whatever change you want.

The second part is growth, which is about action. Improving your life is self-love too, because it says you deserve more than a life that is only okay. Many people use acceptance as an excuse to stay unchallenged.

Think of how you love a partner. Their annoying habits do not make you love them less. But if one habit hurt their health, you would help them change it. You would not judge them harshly, and you would want the best for them. Self-love turns the same care on yourself.

Your feelings are your vibe

King’s idea: your feelings send out a signal, and life sends back what matches it, so you can start feeling good before you get what you want.

One summer at university, King had no money and could not afford a holiday with his friends. He read The Secret, a book about the Law of Attraction: what you think about, you bring about. He tried to think positively about the £500 he needed. A letter came from the tax office, saying he might have overpaid tax, and after a few weeks a cheque for £800 arrived. He flew out with his friends, and he became a believer.

The results were hit and miss, though. Staying positive is hard when things go wrong. Anger often took over for him, and he swung between good periods and very bad ones. He says the missing piece was the Law of Vibration.

The idea starts with atoms. Everything is made of them, he says, and every atom is a little vibration. Your thoughts, words, feelings and actions are vibrations too. Two tuning forks set to the same pitch show the idea: strike one, and the other hums on its own. You attract what matches your own vibration. Your emotions are the best signal of it. You can pretend to be positive, but your feelings show the truth. Acting like someone who feels good can lift you, but covering up anger is not the same.

This is a belief, not a proven law. Critics point to a lack of scientific evidence, he admits, and some call it pseudoscience. He says it does no harm, and that his own experience counts for more than numbers.

Joy and love are the highest feelings, he says. Hatred, anger and despair are the lowest. Most people think they will feel good once they get what they want. You can feel good right now, he says, and that is where it starts.

Turn bad feelings around

Do not bury a bad feeling. Work through it in five steps, and it turns into a better one.

Many people try to wipe out negative thoughts and move on. This fails, he says, because you are only tricking yourself. A buried feeling can come back the next time something similar happens. So he teaches a method, and his client Sarah shows it.

Sarah had been messaging a new love interest, and then he stopped replying. She sat by her phone and decided that no one could be interested in her because she was ugly. That thought made her sad.

Identify means naming the feeling, and digging until you find the ones underneath. Challenge means asking whether the thought is true. Ask big questions too, such as “Does this mean I’ll never be happy?” They push you to dig deeper. Sarah saw that one man not texting did not decide her whole life. Understand means asking what the feeling says about a need you have. Replace means swapping in a thought that helps, backed by a memory of a time you felt worthy and loved. Visualize means picturing yourself handling this feeling in the future, again and again.

The book gives three more ways to lift a mood:

GratitudePicture your life without the thing you are thankful for, so you feel it and do not only say it.
MeditationHe practiced 15 minutes a day for a year, and he felt angry far less often.
Time outSpend time alone or in nature to recharge.
