The 15 Invaluable Laws of Growth

A summary of the book by John C. Maxwell

Growth is a choice

You reach your potential by growing on purpose, every day, and fifteen laws show you how.

John C. Maxwell is an American speaker and leadership author. In 2012 he wrote this book for anyone who wants to be better than they are today, leader or not. You will take away how to start, how to keep going, and how to use hard times.

Grow on purpose

Growth does not happen by itself. You have to choose it, and start today.

GUESS FIRST

Tap an answer

Five frogs sit on a log. Four of them decide to jump off. How many frogs are left on the log?

In 1972 John Maxwell was twenty-four. He had just been offered the best church in his denomination, a large group of churches, and he knew the job was too big for him. Over lunch, a salesman named Curt Kampmeier asked him if he had a plan to grow. He could list his work and his goals. He had no plan to get better. That showed him a flaw in his approach: hard work was not a plan.

Kampmeier sold a growth kit for $799, almost a month’s pay. Saving for it took Maxwell and his wife six months. The kit taught attitude, goals, discipline, measurement and consistency. After a year of steady growth, he says, he began to pass some of his heroes. His belief: focus on growth and you will always hit goals.

His father often used the frog riddle. Most people decide and do not act. The rule behind it is the Law of Diminishing Intent: the longer you put off something you should do now, the less likely you are ever to do it. The book lists eight gaps that keep people from starting. Four of them:

  1. The Assumption Gap. You think you will grow just by living. You will not.
  2. The Timing Gap. You wait for the right time. Waiting makes starting less likely.
  3. The Perfection Gap. You look for the best way first. You see the road as you move.
  4. The Inspiration Gap. You wait to feel like it. The feeling comes after you start.

Know yourself

To grow, know where you stand, believe you are worth the effort, and stop to think.

Maxwell says you need two facts to grow: where you are now, and where you want to go. It is like the dot on a map that says you are here. There is a catch. You must know yourself to grow, but you must grow to know yourself. So explore both at once, and start with what you love to do.

  1. The Law of Awareness. Know your strengths and passions, then act on them every day.
  2. The Law of the Mirror. See value in yourself, so you can add value to yourself.
  3. The Law of Reflection. Pause to think, so growth can catch up with you.

Johnnetta McSwain grew up believing she had no worth. By thirty she had dropped out of school, and she had two sons and no full-time job. On her thirtieth birthday she looked in the mirror and did not like what she saw. She decided to change. She missed the pass mark on her GED, a high school equivalency test, by half a point. She retook it and passed. She moved to Atlanta, and at thirty-three began college. Each semester she tried on a cap and gown in the campus bookstore, and looked in the mirror to picture her graduation. She went on to earn a master’s degree in social work. Her story shows that adding value to yourself and seeing your own value feed each other.

The fix starts with how you talk to yourself. Be your own encourager. Compare yourself only with the person you were before, never with others. And add value to other people, because it is hard to feel bad about yourself while you help someone.

Dev is learning to code. After each practice night he tells himself he is slow, and he checks how far ahead his classmates are. What should he do?

Reflection is stopping to think about what happened. “Experience is not the best teacher. Evaluated experience is!” Life gives the test first and the lesson after, but only if you pause to learn it. Try ten to thirty minutes at the end of each day. Write your answers down, because you often think differently after you write.

Try this. This week, count how often you think something good or bad about yourself. Use your phone or an index card.

Set up your days

Growth lasts when your days, your surroundings and your plan all support it.

Maxwell had a loving home and a career he enjoyed. Yet less than ten years in, his denomination, the group of churches he belonged to, was already treating him as the best in its class. He knew he was not as good as they thought, and that the group was small. In his words, “If you’re always at the head of the class, then you’re in the wrong class.” So he left for a bigger pond, with his father’s support.

In 1975 an author he admired taught him the Hot Poker Principle on a flight. Put an iron poker, a rod for stirring a fire, next to the fire and it gets hot. People are the same. If your surroundings are cold, you are cold. So be around great people, go to great places and read great books.

  1. The Law of Consistency. “Motivation gets you going, but discipline keeps you growing.” Start with small goals and do them every day.
  2. The Law of Environment. Change yourself and your surroundings together. Changing only one is slower.
  3. The Law of Design. Use systems, which are steps you can repeat. Big things take longer and cost more than we hope, so plan with double the time and money.
