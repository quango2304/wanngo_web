A summary of the book by Mrs. Hinch

Cleaning as a way to feel calm

A home you love and look after can calm a worried mind, because cleaning gives your thoughts a job, a finish line and a small win.

Sophie Hinchliffe, known online as Mrs Hinch, shares her cleaning on Instagram. She calls cleaning "hinching". She says she is not a professional cleaner, but she does live with anxiety, and grabbing a cloth calms her more than anything else. Her book mixes cleaning tips with her own story. You will take away ways to make your home feel good and chores feel lighter.

What you'll learn

Then a short quiz, and the key takeaways on one page.

Make your home yours

A home is for living in, so fill it with things you love and look after them, instead of decorating for visitors.

Mrs Hinch warns against making your home a show home just to impress other people. She thinks the better test is what you like. A friend may pop over for a cup of tea once a week, but you live there day in, day out.

Her own style is grey and white. Some people find that boring, but she loves it, and says she found herself through her home. Years ago she would have decorated to please others. Her mum's home is full of oatmeal and brown shades, and a friend's is green and brown. She likes them too. Every person is different, so every home is.

She also enjoys looking after what she owns. She bought a big white wooden candlestick for £6.99 because the metal dish on top had broken off. She glued it back with superglue, and the repair made her smile. She could buy a new one, but asks why you would, when you are already happy with what you have. Her storage footstool, called an ottoman, came from a boot sale, a market for second-hand things. She took the nails out, painted it and covered the seat in new fabric. She paid pennies and says it is one of the best things she owns. So care and effort make you fond of things, and a cared-for home can bring more joy than a new one.

Cleaning calms a worried mind

When worry starts, give your hands a job. A task you can finish takes your mind off its spiral.

Mrs Hinch says she has always been a worrier. She lives with anxiety, nerves and panic attacks. She says grabbing a favorite cloth and getting busy is the thing that calms her mind more than anything else. Her followers are called "Hinchers".

It began when a worry or a deep thought made her chest feel tight with anxiety. She would jump up, turn the music on loud and grab the nearest cloth, mop or vacuum. She was no longer just sitting and thinking. She was doing something, and her thoughts stopped running away with her.

Two things seem to help. The first is focus: when you put your mind on what you are doing, you stop focusing on what makes you uneasy. The second is the finish. She likes to leave a room and think, "I've done all of that."

She felt this most when she could not clean. In hospital, ill with a blood clot, she missed it, because it keeps her mind focused. Her mind kept going round and round, so she wiped the small table over her bed with baby wipes her mum brought in, and kept going until the pack was almost empty. So even one small table gave her mind something to hold.

She presents it as one way to manage worry, not a cure for everything. Some people go to the gym or bake cakes. For her it is cleaning. She says she does not have OCD, and her panic was not caused by a messy house.

Make the chore fun

A silly name and a strong beat make a chore easier to start, and the beat helps you keep going.

Mrs Hinch gave her yellow kitchen cloth a name as a joke, and a follower wrote back, "call it Buddy". Soon other people were sharing pictures of their own named cloths. Now her tools are a family. Sharon the Shark is her upright vacuum, Dave the Duster is her fluffy duster and Vera is her spray mop.

Her favorite is Minkeh, a cleaning pad with a rough white side and a green honeycomb side. She says the name the way her dad does, in a Northern accent. She found a bathrobe made for a toy elf, and it fit Minkeh perfectly. People loved it, and the hashtag #minkonthesink began, with fans dressing up their own pads. One mother filmed her daughter, aged almost six, asking for a Minkeh for her birthday. So a silly name turned a chore into something people shared.

Music works the same way. She says the beat of a song drives her on to do a bit more, like giving the treadmill another five minutes at the gym when a banging track comes on. With a Beyoncé track on, she dances round the room with Vera and sings into the handle as if it were a mic. She also pairs songs with her tools, often by a silly link.
