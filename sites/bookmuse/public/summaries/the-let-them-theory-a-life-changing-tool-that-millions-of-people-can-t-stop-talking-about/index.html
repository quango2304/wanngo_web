The Let Them Theory

A summary of the book by Mel Robbins

8 lessons · 19 min · quiz and takeaways

Read it lesson by lessonGet the app

Two words and a choice

You cannot control other people, so say Let Them to let go of them, then say Let Me to choose what you do next.

Mel Robbins is a speaker, podcaster and author. She found the idea at her son’s prom. She shared it in a 60-second video, and more than 15 million people watched it in a day. Her 2024 book turns it into a tool you can use with stress, opinions, feelings, friends and love.

What you'll learn

Then a short quiz, and the key takeaways on one page.

Let Them, then Let Me

Let Them frees you from what you cannot control. Let Me is what you do next, and the theory only works with both.

Robbins found the idea at her son’s prom. Twenty teenagers in formal clothes planned to eat at a tiny taco shop in pouring rain, with no reservation. Other parents rushed in. She searched for restaurants. Her daughter Kendall took her arm and said, “Let them.” So she handed her son forty dollars and watched him run out into the rain. The stress faded, because it came from trying to run his evening.

People have a hardwired need for control, she says. But you can never control another adult. You can only control your own thoughts, your actions and how you respond.

Later she saw photos of a weekend trip that her friends took without her. She felt hurt and told herself she had done something wrong. Saying Let Them helped, but only half.

Let Them is the first half. Let Me is the second: you take charge of your next move. She asked herself if she had done her part. She had barely seen these friends for a long time and had invited no one. So she chose to reach out, because she missed a few of them.

Let Them is not “letting it go”. You are releasing your grip, not swallowing your feelings. It is also no excuse to ghost people, skip hard talks or stay where you are hurt. If it leaves you lonely, Robbins says you are using it wrong: you left out Let Me.

Switch off the stress alarm

When other people stress you, say Let Them to pause, then Let Me, take a breath and choose your next move.

On a plane, the man behind Robbins coughed without covering his mouth. She had big speeches coming and could not afford to get sick. She asked him politely to cover up. He nodded and kept coughing, and she stayed angry and got no work done.

So the stress left once she stopped working on him and worked on herself. She wrote, “Problem solved.”

Dr. Aditi Neurukar, a Harvard Medical School doctor, told Robbins that stress is a state of the brain. The prefrontal cortex, the part that plans and decides, steps back. The amygdala, your alarm for fight, flight or freeze, takes over. That is why you snap or send the angry text. Her own view is that Let Them tells the alarm to switch off. Dr. Aditi adds that a slow, deep breath helps calm it.

Not every moment needs a response. She asks herself if it will still bother her in an hour. If it will, she should do something. If it will matter in a week or a year, she definitely should.

Let them think what they think

You cannot control what anyone thinks of you, so give people the freedom to judge and live in a way that makes you proud.

When Robbins began to speak for a living, other speakers gave her a formula. Build a simple website, collect testimonials, and post about speaking on social media. She did the first two. It took her two years to start posting.

She feared her friends would think, “Who does she think she is?” She wrote hundreds of draft posts and left them in her drafts. When she did post, she checked the likes and deleted the post if there were few. So fear of other people’s opinions held back the career she wanted, for years.

Most advice says to stop caring, Robbins says, but not how. Her way is to give people the freedom to think badly of you, because you cannot stop them. She points out that people have about 70,000 thoughts a day, and you cannot even control your own.

A bad thought is not a verdict on all of you. She has bad thoughts about her husband, her dog and her children every day, and still loves them. Her children list her faults too, and still love her. Two things can be true.

Then ask what would make you proud. Go to a friend’s party because you want to be a good friend, not to look like one, she says. Someone is always disappointed. Do not let it be you.
