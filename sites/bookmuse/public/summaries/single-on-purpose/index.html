Single On Purpose

A summary of the book by John Kim

7 lessons · 17 min · quiz and takeaways

Read it lesson by lessonGet the app

Know yourself before you look for someone

You cannot build a healthy bond with anyone until you build one with yourself, so use your single time, and your time inside a relationship, to know who you are.

John Kim is a therapist in Los Angeles who blogs as The Angry Therapist. After his divorce he chose, for the first time, to be alone. He calls that being single on purpose: using time on your own to build a bond with yourself. He says the thousands of people he coached often felt something was wrong with them for being single. Many had also lost themselves in a partner, which he calls codependency: leaning on someone else to feel that you matter. The book has three acts: The Self (connect back to you), Relationship Residue (let go of the past) and The New You.

Single is not a defect

Feeling lonely is a feeling, not a fact about your worth. Accept that you may not find a partner, then go and live.

GUESS FIRST

Tap an answer

In 2017 the US census counted the adults in America who were not married. What share of all adults was that?

Kim says every one of the thousands of singles he coached believed they could not be happy without a partner. They felt incomplete. He blames what we take in from movies, ads and social media. Few stories show someone who never finds love and is still happy.

The loneliness is real. You miss the talk after work and the waffles on Saturday morning. It is like feeling tired when you have not slept. The trouble starts when "I feel lonely" turns into "I am lonely." Then you decide something is wrong with you.

His answer is radical acceptance: say yes to life as it is. Picture never finding a partner, and stay with that thought for a while. Then accept that you might not. That does not mean giving up on love or staying home. It means your world will not end, so you can stop waiting. His clients who did this built a business, a stronger body and friendships. Some took dance classes. He now asks "Where am I going?" before "Who is going with me?"

QUICK CHECK

Tap an answer

Dana has been single since spring. On Friday night a wave of loneliness hits, and she wonders if she will ever find anyone. What should she do?

Try this. Ask yourself what would happen if you never found a partner, and let the answer be okay. Then do one thing you have put off: a class, a trip or a hobby.

Treat yourself like someone you love

Self-care means treating yourself like someone you love, in what you do and in what you say.

GUESS FIRST

Tap an answer

Which matters more for how you end up treating yourself: what you do for yourself, or how you talk to yourself?

Kim once drove to the beach after a shift counseling teenagers in recovery. Alone on the sand, he heard a voice in his head call him an idiot who never finishes anything. He got up and ran down the shore, plowing through the waves. He stayed all day, and he bought himself an expensive meal on the pier while he thought about what he liked. He calls it his first true date with himself. He was already fit and busy, but he had been ignoring his feelings. For once, he says, he was not running from himself.

Self-care is not bubble baths or brunch. It means looking after yourself every day as you would someone you love. You stop putting yourself last. You stop taking on everything. You say no. It feels selfish at first, so it takes thousands of small acts. It is a lifestyle, not a checklist. Kim says how you talk to yourself matters even more than how you treat yourself, because your words shape your actions more than your actions shape your words.

He adds that you should stop ordering yourself to love yourself. That adds pressure. Plan a first date with yourself instead: a walk, a workout or a coffee on a wall. What counts is your attention. No phone, no to-do list, and no replaying why you have not met someone.

QUICK CHECK

Tap an answer

Priya's friend misses a big deadline and cries about it, and Priya is kind and honest with her. Then Priya misses one of her own and calls herself lazy and useless. What should she do?

Try this. List how you treat and talk to the people close to you. Then ask where you do not treat yourself that way. Those are the things to work on.

Body, mind and soul

Your bond with yourself has three parts, body, mind and soul. All three must pump, or you stall.

Kim pictures them as the three pistons of an engine. If one is off, the engine is stuck. If all three get what they need, you move forward, and in time you start to like yourself.

  1. Body: accept it, notice how it feels, and move in ways you enjoy and need.
  2. Mind: notice your thoughts, question them, and watch for patterns that repeat. Do it daily.
  3. Soul: do what makes you feel alive, and do not save it for when you are successful.

The soul is the part we forget. A client in her fifties felt flat, though nothing was wrong on paper. Kim asked when she had felt most alive. In high school, she said, with red hair and an electric guitar. He asked her to buy one and pick it up now and then. It made her feel heard and invincible. He asked where else in her life she lacked that feeling, and she said everywhere. She then began to give herself that feeling: she spoke up at work and said what she wanted in her relationship. Some friends drifted away, and she let them go. The guitar was never the point. The feeling was.

QUICK CHECK

Tap an answer

Marco trains six days a week and his body is strong. At night he replays the same worries, and nothing in his life moves forward. What should he work on next?

Try this. Ask yourself when you felt most alive. Not happiest: most alive. Then write down one step you could take this week to bring that feeling back.
