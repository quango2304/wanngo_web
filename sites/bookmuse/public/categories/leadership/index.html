Leadership book summaries

20 books, most read first. Each one is a short course of visual lessons.

  1. Can't Hurt MeDavid Goggins · 19 minMost people quit long before their limit, and you can move that limit by telling yourself the truth, doing hard things on purpose and learning from every failure.
  2. The PrinceNiccolò Machiavelli · 16 minA ruler keeps power by judging people as they really are, relying on his own strength, never making his people hate him, and preparing in calm times for the day luck turns.
  3. MindsetCarol S. Dweck · 19 minYour abilities can be carved in stone, or they can be grown. Which one you believe shapes how you handle challenge, failure and effort, and you can change it.
  4. Playing to WinA.G. Lafley and Roger L. Martin · 19 minStrategy is not a vision or a plan. It is a set of five connected choices about how you will win, and you can test those choices by asking “What would have to be true?”
  5. Team of TeamsStanley McChrystal, Tantum Collins, David Silverman and Chris Fussell · 20 minA large organization built to be efficient will lose in a fast, tangled world, unless it learns to work as a team of teams: everyone sees the whole picture, and the people closest to a problem decide.
  6. Turn the Ship Around!L. David Marquet · 20 minIn Marquet's account, people treated as followers act like followers; give them control, plus the competence and the clarity to use it, and they act like leaders.
  7. Invent & WanderJeff Bezos and Walter Isaacson · 17 minBezos built Amazon by thinking in years, serving customers instead of rivals, and inventing through many bold bets, and he tried to keep it Day One as the company grew.
  8. The 48 Laws of PowerRobert Greene · 19 minPower is a game that everyone plays, and Greene says you do better when you see how it works: keep a calm face, hide your aims, manage your image, learn what people want, stop in time, and stay hard to pin down.
  9. The 7 Habits of Highly Effective PeopleStephen R. Covey · 19 minLasting change works from the inside out: change how you see things, then build seven habits that take you from depending on others, to standing on your own, to working well with others.
  10. PresenceAmy Cuddy · 17 minPresence is being able to show your real self in the moments that scare you. It comes from feeling personally powerful, and small nudges, in your mind and your body, can help you find it.
  11. Power QuestionsAndrew Sobel, Jerold Panas · 18 minThe right question at the right moment can do more than any answer: it builds trust, wins business and moves people.
  12. The Charisma MythOlivia Fox Cabane · 17 minCharisma is not a gift you are born with. It is behavior that shows presence, power and warmth, and anyone can learn it by starting inside.
  13. The Diary of a CEOSteven Bartlett · 18 minGreat things are built by people who follow a few lasting laws about themselves, their story, their thinking and their team.
  14. Leaders Eat LastSimon Sinek · 17 minPeople give their best when they feel safe with each other, and a leader's job is to build that safety, even when it costs the leader something.
  15. Scaling Up ExcellenceRobert I. Sutton and Huggy Rao · 18 minSpreading excellence from a few people to many is slow, hands-on work, done one person and one small change at a time.
  16. ScrumJeff Sutherland · 18 minA plan made in advance is only a guess, so Scrum works in short cycles, ends each one with finished work, and fixes whatever slows the team down.
  17. Start with WhySimon Sinek · 17 minBuyers choose the reason behind what you do, so great leaders start with their purpose and work outward.
  18. The 80/20 CEOBill Canady · 18 minA business gets stronger when its leaders find the small share of customers and products that bring most of the profit, put their effort there, and run a simple hundred-day system to get started.
  19. The Art of WarSun Tzu · 18 minThe best win costs the least: you prepare, you learn what the enemy will do, and you win before the fight starts, or without a fight at all.
  20. The Great CEO WithinMatt Mochary · 17 minA great CEO is trained, not born: fix your own habits first, then run a simple written system that keeps a growing team pointed the same way.
