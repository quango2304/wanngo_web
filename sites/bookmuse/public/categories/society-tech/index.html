Society & Tech book summaries

35 books, most read first. Each one is a short course of visual lessons.

  1. CosmosCarl Sagan · 25 minWe live on a small world in a vast and ancient Cosmos, we come from it, and science is how we found out.
  2. Why Nations FailDaron Acemoglu and James A. Robinson · 22 minNations are rich or poor because of their institutions, the rules that decide who can work, invest and hold power. Rules that share power make a country rich. Rules that let a few take from the many keep it poor.
  3. Guns, Germs, and SteelJared Diamond · 24 minPeoples ended up with different power because of the land, plants and animals they started with, not because of any difference in their minds.
  4. Surrounded by IdiotsThomas Erikson · 17 minIn Erikson's model, people act in four main styles, and you get through to anyone by meeting them on their terms, not yours.
  5. Astrophysics for People in a HurryNeil deGrasse Tyson · 19 minThe same laws of physics work everywhere, so by reading every kind of light we have traced the universe from a pinpoint to us, and most of it is still dark.
  6. Principles for Dealing with the Changing World OrderRay Dalio · 22 minEmpires, their money and their markets rise and fall in one repeating Big Cycle, so the smart move is to learn its pattern and protect yourself from the worst case.
  7. The Selfish GeneRichard Dawkins · 25 minEvolution works on genes, not on groups: genes that copy themselves well spread, bodies are the machines they build, and kindness shows up among relatives and between players who meet again.
  8. Tuesdays with MorrieMitch Albom · 18 minFacing death honestly shows what matters in life: love, giving, attention and forgiveness, not money, status or hurry.
  9. Automate the Boring Stuff with PythonAl Sweigart · 20 minA short program can do in seconds the repetitive computer jobs that eat your hours, and you do not have to be a software engineer to write one.
  10. Born a CrimeTrevor Noah · 20 minApartheid made Trevor Noah’s birth a crime and tried to keep people apart, and a mother who loved hard, taught him to think and found the cracks in unjust rules gave him ways to cross every line.
  11. OutliersMalcolm Gladwell · 20 minSuccessful people did not rise on talent and hard work alone. Hidden chances, the year they were born and the culture they came from helped make them.
  12. Women Who Run With the WolvesClarissa Pinkola Estés · 23 minEvery woman has a wild, instinctive self that knows what is true and what she needs. Being trained to be tame buries it, and old stories show how to dig it up and live with it.
  13. A People's History of the United StatesHoward Zinn · 24 minTold from below, American history shows a few powerful people using the nation, the law and war to protect their wealth, and ordinary people who pushed back again and again and sometimes won.
  14. A Room of One's OwnVirginia Woolf · 18 minA woman needs money of her own and a room with a lock on the door if she is to write fiction, because a free mind needs both, and for centuries women had neither.
  15. FactfulnessHans Rosling, Ola Rosling and Anna Rosling Rönnlund · 20 minAlmost everyone sees the world as more frightening and hopeless than it is, because ten built-in instincts pull our thinking toward drama.
  16. FreakonomicsSteven D. Levitt and Stephen J. Dubner · 19 minPeople respond to incentives, and when you follow the data, the popular story is often wrong.
  17. The Fire Next TimeJames Baldwin · 19 minJames Baldwin says America's race problem grows out of white fear and white innocence, that hating back harms the hater too, and that Black and white Americans can only be free together.
  18. The Sum of UsHeather McGhee · 20 minThe belief that one group gains only when another loses is a false story, and it has drained the shared goods that most Americans depend on.
  19. A Brief History of TimeStephen Hawking · 20 minThe universe runs on laws we can understand, and joining the science of the very big with the science of the very small may show how it all began.
  20. Capital in the 21st CenturyThomas Piketty · 21 minWhen wealth earns more each year than the economy grows, money from the past pulls ahead of pay from the present, and inequality rises unless people choose to act.
  21. What If?Randall Munroe · 19 minA silly question, answered seriously with simple numbers, shows you how the world really works.
  22. The Design of Everyday ThingsDon Norman · 20 minWhen a thing is hard to use, the design failed, not you. Good design shows what to do, answers every action, and expects people to slip.
  23. The Omnivore's DilemmaMichael Pollan · 19 minMost of what we eat comes from one cheap crop, corn, and its real cost is hidden. Knowing where your food comes from helps you choose better.
  24. Hillbilly ElegyJ.D. Vance · 18 minA poor town's troubles are not only about lost jobs. They also come from chaos at home and a belief that your choices do not matter, and one steady, loving person can change a child's life.
  25. Refactoring UIAdam Wathan, Steve Schoger · 20 minA good-looking interface needs no artistic talent: build one real feature at a time, choose from short lists of values, and make what matters stand out.
  26. Talking to StrangersMalcolm Gladwell · 19 minWe misread strangers because we start by believing them, trust faces and manner that do not show feelings, and ignore the setting, so we should meet them with caution and humility.
  27. InfluenceRobert B. Cialdini · 19 minMost of our yes decisions come from a few mental shortcuts. Each shortcut usually points the right way, and each can be used on us by someone who wants a yes.
  28. Biohack Your BrainKristen Willeumier · 17 minAt any age, you can improve your brain with everyday habits: moving, eating well, drinking water, calming stress, thinking kindly and learning new things.
  29. A Short History of Nearly EverythingBill Bryson · 23 minWe know the story of the universe, the Earth and life because people kept asking "how do they know that?", and the answer shows how lucky we are to be here.
  30. NexusYuval Noah Harari · 19 minInformation does not mainly show us the truth. It connects people into networks, so every network needs ways to fix its own mistakes, and AI is the first member of those networks that is not human.
  31. SapiensYuval Noah Harari · 25 minHumans rule the world because we can believe in shared stories, and those stories let millions of strangers work together.
  32. Stolen FocusJohann Hari · 17 minHari argues that our focus is falling apart because of twelve forces in the way we live, so fixing it takes personal steps and changes we make together.
  33. Ten Arguments to Delete Your Social Media Account Right NowJaron Lanier · 19 minSocial media earns its money by changing what you do for customers you cannot see, so Lanier argues you should delete your accounts and find out who you are without them.
  34. The Art of WarSun Tzu · 18 minThe best win costs the least: you prepare, you learn what the enemy will do, and you win before the fight starts, or without a fight at all.
  35. 21 Lessons for the 21st CenturyYuval Noah Harari · 20 minThe big stories that explained the world are failing just as new technology arrives, so the best defense is to see through stories, admit what you do not know, and know your own mind.
