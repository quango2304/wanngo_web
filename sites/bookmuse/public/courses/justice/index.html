Justice

Course · 10 parts · 2 h 10 min · By Bookmuse

Ten short parts on the big question of what is fair: making the most people happy, owning ourselves, what money shouldn't buy, doing right for its own sake, choosing rules behind a veil, who deserves what, and the ties we never chose. Each part uses everyday cases.

Based on the ideas in Michael Sandel's Justice.

The parts

  1. 1Hard casesBookmuse · 12 minWe judge what is fair with three ideas, and we sharpen each one by testing it on hard cases.
  2. 2The greatest happinessBookmuse · 12 minBentham said the right act is the one that brings the most happiness to everyone it touches, and that simple idea has two big problems.
  3. 3Not all pleasures are equalBookmuse · 12 minMill tried to save the greatest happiness idea by adding freedom and quality, and critics say each rescue leans on a value beyond happiness.
  4. 4Do we own ourselves?Bookmuse · 13 minLibertarians say you own yourself, so what you earn is yours and only a thin state is fair, but the idea is hard to follow to its edge.
  5. 5What money shouldn't buyBookmuse · 13 minLetting money decide can go wrong in two ways: the choice may not be free, and the thing sold may lose its meaning.
  6. 6Duty and motiveBookmuse · 14 minFor Kant, an act is good when you do it because it is right, and acting on that reason is what it means to be free.
  7. 7Is lying ever right?Bookmuse · 13 minKant says a lie is wrong even to a murderer at the door, a defender finds a line between a lie and a misleading truth, and Kant tests a just law by an agreement that never happened.
  8. 8Behind the veilBookmuse · 14 minThe fair rules for a society are the ones we would pick if we did not know where we would end up in it.
  9. 9Who deserves whatBookmuse · 14 minWho deserves a place or a prize depends on what the thing is for, so arguments about fairness turn into arguments about purpose and honor.
  10. 10What we owe each otherBookmuse · 14 minOn the view this part ends with, we may owe each other more than we ever agreed to, and a fair society cannot avoid asking what a good life together is.
